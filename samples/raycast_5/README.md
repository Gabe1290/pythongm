# 2.5 D — Level 5

The fifth first-person level, and the first with **height**: a staircase you
can climb, a gallery to walk along, a ledge to jump off, and a pillar too high
to get over. Until now every raycast room was flat — this one shows how to
build stairs out of ordinary objects.

## How to play

| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| **↑** / **↓** | Walk forward / backward |
| **←** / **→** | Turn left / right |

You start at the bottom, facing the staircase. Walk straight ahead: the view
rises step by step as you climb. At the top, turn right and follow the gallery
to the **flag**. Mind the **monster** patrolling the lower hall — if it catches
you, you start again.

## How stairs are made

A step is an ordinary object that is **not solid**, with one action in its
**Create** event:

**Set Floor Height** (3D View) — `height` in room pixels, plus an optional
**Top Texture** and **Side Texture** (blank = use the floor and wall textures).

Every grid cell the step's sprite covers becomes raised floor of that height.
This sample uses one object per height — `obj_step_8`, `obj_step_16`,
`obj_step_24`, `obj_step_32` (the gallery) and `obj_step_48` (the pillar) —
placed on the grid like walls. In the room editor they are just grey squares
(lighter = higher); only the first-person view draws them as steps.

Objects stand on the floor of their cell: put the flag on the gallery and it
is drawn up there; walk behind a step and it hides the lower part of what is
behind it.

## How climbing works

Two settings on **Enable Raycast View** (in `obj_person`'s Create event):

- **Wall Height** = `3` cells. The usual 1.5 leaves only a little headroom
  above the eye; raised floors need taller walls and a higher ceiling.
- **Max Step** = `12` pixels: the highest step you can walk up. The 8-px
  steps are fine; the 48-px pillar blocks you like a wall (you slide along
  it). Walking **down** is always allowed — step off the gallery anywhere.

Your view rises smoothly as you climb and falls when you step off a ledge.

The same rule applies to **everything that moves**, not just the player: the
monster cannot climb the pillar either. A refused step fires no event, so
each moving instance gets a variable instead:

| Variable | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `raycast_step_blocked` | `1` on a frame when a step was too high, else `0` |
| `raycast_floor_z` | the floor height under it, in pixels |

The monster's **End Step** event uses the first one:
*Test Variable* `raycast_step_blocked` = `1` → *Reverse Horizontal*. That is
how it turns back at the pillar. You could use `raycast_floor_z` to test
"am I on the gallery?" (`raycast_floor_z` = `32`).

## The minimap

The minimap (top left, `draw_minimap` in `obj_person`'s Draw event) shades
raised cells by height, so the staircase and the gallery show up as steps of
grey, and marks the flag in cyan.

## Project structure

| Object | Role |
|---|---|
| `obj_person` | The player and the camera: controls, view settings, minimap, collisions with the monster and the flag |
| `obj_wall` | Solid walls around the room |
| `obj_step_8` … `obj_step_48` | Steps, the gallery and the pillar (Set Floor Height) |
| `obj_monster` | Patrols the lower hall, turns back at walls and at the pillar |
| `obj_goal` | The flag on the gallery |

The room is generated by `tools/gen_raycast_5_stairs.py`, so it can be
rebuilt or changed in one place (the test suite checks the files match it).

## Things to tweak

- Set **Max Step** to `24` on Enable Raycast View: you can now climb onto
  the staircase from the side, straight from the hall onto the 16-px step.
- Change `obj_step_16`'s **Set Floor Height** to `30`: from the 8-px step
  that is a 22-px climb, more than Max Step — the staircase is now blocked.
- Set **Wall Height** back to `1.5`: there is no longer room for a 32-px
  gallery under the ceiling, so every floor higher than 20 px is clamped to
  20 px (a warning is logged).
- Give `obj_step_32` a **Top Texture** to make the gallery floor look
  different from the hall.
