# 2.5 D — Level 6: the junction

The sixth first-person level, and the one that fools the eye. The 2.5D view
cannot put a floor above another floor, so games of this kind (Doom and its
descendants) cheat: **a low ceiling** hides what is above, and **a seamless
jump into another room** makes two rooms feel like one place. This level uses
both.

## How to play

| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| **↑** / **↓** | Walk forward / backward |
| **←** / **→** | Turn left / right |

You start in a hall with a **gallery** (a platform) at the far end, reached by
a small staircase. Find the **corridor** on the east side — its ceiling is
much lower than the hall's — and walk to the **flags** at its end. Somewhere
on the way you change room without noticing. Then turn round and walk back to
the west: the hall behind you is not the one you left.

## Low ceilings

A **Set Ceiling Height** action (3D View) in the **Create** event of a
non-solid object lowers the ceiling over every grid cell its sprite covers:

- **Height** — in room pixels above the ground, like Set Floor Height
  (`40` here: the eye is 24 px up, so the corridor feels low).
- **Ceiling Texture** — optional (blank = the camera's ceiling texture).

This sample uses one object, `obj_low` (invisible), placed on every corridor
cell — and, in the second room, on every hall cell: the hall there is *under
the gallery*, so its ceiling is low. A ceiling is never lower than the floor
plus the eye plus 4 px, so you can always stand in a cell; where a lower
ceiling starts you see a **lintel** (the wall texture hanging down).

The ceiling height is readable too: `raycast_ceiling_z` on any moving instance
(the ceiling over it), next to `raycast_floor_z`.

## The seamless jump

**Go to Room Seamlessly** (3D View) takes two settings:

- **Room** — where to go.
- **Object** — a *marker*: one instance of this object placed in **each**
  room, at the same spot of the geometry the two rooms share.

The camera arrives in the other room at the same distance from the marker,
facing the same way, at the same speed, with the same held keys and the same
view height — and no frame is ever drawn at the new room's own start. Unlike
**Go to Room**, there is no fade and no pause.

Here the marker is `obj_junction` (invisible), at the middle of the corridor
in both rooms. The jump is triggered by an invisible **gate** object across the
corridor (`obj_gate_a`, in the first room); the second room has a line of
`obj_gate_b` across its hall that jumps back. Keep a gate away from the spot
where the *other* room lets you arrive — otherwise you would jump back at once.

For the illusion to work the two rooms must look the same around the marker
as far as the player can see: here the corridor is identical, and only what is
behind you differs.

## Project structure

| Object | Role |
|---|---|
| `obj_person` | The player and the camera: controls, view settings, the two gates, the flag |
| `obj_wall` | Solid walls |
| `obj_low` | Lowers the ceiling over its cells (Set Ceiling Height) |
| `obj_step_8` … `obj_step_32` | The staircase and the gallery (Set Floor Height) |
| `obj_junction` | The marker, in both rooms at the same spot |
| `obj_gate_a`, `obj_gate_b` | Invisible triggers that run Go to Room Seamlessly |
| `obj_goal` | The flags at the corridor's end, in both rooms |

The rooms are generated by `tools/gen_raycast_6_junction.py`, so they can be
rebuilt or changed in one place (the test suite checks the files match it).

## Things to tweak

- Change `obj_low`'s **Height** to `60`: a roomier corridor, and a smaller
  lintel at its mouth.
- Give `obj_low` a **Ceiling Texture** (any sprite) to texture the low ceilings.
- Move `obj_junction` in **one** room only: the jump now shifts you sideways.
  Both markers must be at the same spot.
