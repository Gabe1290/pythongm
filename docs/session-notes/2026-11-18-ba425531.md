> Answer the following question, do not implement:
> Is it possible to tunnel under an existing platform that isat the top of some stairs?

### Claude

No, not with the current engine. You can only go around a platform, not under it.

**Why:** the 2.5D view stores exactly **one floor height per grid cell**. Set Floor Height records a single height for each cell, and an empty cell is at ground level. Once a cell belongs to a platform at 80 px, there's no "ground under the platform" for that cell. The ceiling is also a single height for the whole room, set by the Wall Height setting on Enable Raycast View, so there are no per-cell ceilings that could form a tunnel roof. That rules out a floor above a floor, a bridge you can walk under, or a room on top of another room. Classic Doom had the same limit.

**What can be done today, to give the impression of a tunnel:**
- **A passage beside the stairs:** a low corridor that runs along the platform's side, between walls. At walking level it looks like you're going under, although nothing is above you.
- **Two rooms:** walking into a doorway at the foot of the stairs sends you, with Go to Room, to a second room laid out as the space "under" the platform. A small trigger object at the doorway does it.
- **Walls as pillars:** the platform's edge can sit on solid wall cells, so it looks like it's on supports with a dark gap. You still can't walk into the gap.

**What a real tunnel would take:** a change to how the engine stores and draws the level. Each cell would need several floor and ceiling layers, and every one of the three renderers (desktop, HTML5 and Android/iOS), plus climbing and collision, would need to handle them. That would be a large project, much bigger than the stairs work.

## [2026-11-23] User

> Once again, just curious, do not implement:
> How did Doom get around this limit?

### Claude

Mostly it didn't. Doom had the same fundamental limit and worked around it with level design, while later games found real solutions.

**What Doom (1993) could do.** Doom maps are drawn in 2D: every area ("sector") is a shape on a flat map, with one floor height and one ceiling height. Two places can never sit at the same map position at different heights, so no floor above a floor and no real bridge over a walkway. It was more flexible than PyGameMaker's view in two ways:
- Sectors are free-form shapes, not grid cells.
- Each sector has its own ceiling height, not one ceiling for the whole room. That made low tunnels, alcoves and windows easy, which is why its levels feel more three-dimensional than they are.

**How designers hid the limit:**
- **Routing:** paths wind around raised areas instead of going under them. The overlap players think they remember was usually two areas side by side, linked by a long corridor or a lift.
- **Lifts and teleporters:** a lift drops you into an area that appears to be "below" where you were. A teleporter can make the layout feel impossible while the 2D map stays flat.
- **Windows and height differences:** low-ceiling passages next to tall halls, and openings looking down into lower rooms, give a strong sense of stacked space.
- **Engine tricks found later by mappers:** "invisible" or "deep water" sectors, which exploit how the renderer draws missing textures. They fake bridges and swimmable water, but only visually; you still can't walk underneath.

**How later engines solved it:**
- **Build engine** (Duke Nukem 3D, 1996) faked room-over-room by overlapping sectors and silently teleporting the player between them, for example for underwater areas, or by drawing one area through another.
- **Quake** (1996) dropped the 2D map and became truly 3D, so rooms over rooms became normal.
- **Fan-made Doom engines** (Doom Legacy, ZDoom/GZDoom and others) added "3D floors" and portals in the late 1990s and 2000s, so modern Doom mods can have real bridges.

**For PyGameMaker:** two Doom ideas would bring the 2.5D view closer to Doom without real overlap:
- a per-cell ceiling height, to allow low tunnels beside platforms;
- a silent "teleport to a matching position" action, to join two rooms seamlessly.

A walkable tunnel under a platform would need the Quake or 3D-floor approach: several floors per cell, in all three renderers.
