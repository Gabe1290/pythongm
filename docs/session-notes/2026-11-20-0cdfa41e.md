Yes for walls and ceilings. For the floor, only partly: there's one floor texture per camera, with a workaround for patches.

**Walls.** `raycast_6` gives every wall the same look because of one setting: `obj_person`'s **Enable Raycast View** has **Wall Texture = `spr_wall_texture`**. When that's set, it replaces every wall's own sprite ([renderer.py:897](extensions/raycast_2_5d/renderer.py#L897)). To get different walls:
1. Clear **Wall Texture** (leave it blank). Each wall is then drawn with its own object's sprite.
2. Make a second wall object, e.g. `obj_wall_brick`: solid, with its own sprite. Draw the brick art into that sprite, the way `spr_wall_texture` is drawn now.
3. Place the different wall objects wherever you want each look.

One catch: once Wall Texture is blank, `obj_wall` shows its own sprite (`spr_wall`), which is a plain collision block, not wall art. You'll want to repaint it too.

**Ceilings.** These can already vary by area. Use **Set Ceiling Height** on a non-solid object and fill in its **Ceiling Texture**. `obj_low` in `raycast_6` does this, with the texture left blank.

**Floors.**
- **One texture per camera:** **Floor Texture** on Enable Raycast View (`spr_floor` here). In `raycast_6` the camera is `obj_person` in both rooms, so both rooms share it. To give each room its own floor, put Enable Raycast View on a separate controller object in each room, with Camera Object = `obj_person`. That's how `raycast_2` does it.
- **Raised areas:** **Set Floor Height** has **Top Texture** (the top of the step) and **Side Texture** (its front). The step objects in `raycast_6` leave both blank, so they use the room's floor and wall textures.
- **Patches at ground level** (a rug, a puddle): there's no action for this. A step of height 0 is ignored completely, texture included. Workaround: a step with **Height 1** and a Top Texture. It looks flat and you can walk over it, but I haven't tested how it looks in a game.
