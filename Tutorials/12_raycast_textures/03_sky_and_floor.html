Phase 2: Sky and Floor

Draw the Sky

  1. Create a sprite named spr_sky, 256×64 pixels — wide and short, like a strip cut from a panorama
  2. Fill it with sky blue and add a few white clouds, a sun, or mountains along the bottom edge. Save it.
How the sky behaves: it pans sideways as you turn — one full 360° turn slides the whole strip past once — but it does not get bigger or smaller as you walk. That is exactly what a far-away horizon does, and it is how the classic games faked one.

Draw the Floor Tile

  1. Create a sprite named spr_floor, 32×32 pixels — the size of one grid cell
  2. Make a checkerboard, flagstones, or planks. Save it.

The floor tile is repeated once per grid cell, and it lines up with the bottoms of the walls, so the floor never seems to slide.

Turn Both On

When created
Enable Raycast View — … Wall Texture spr_wall_texture Sky Texture spr_sky Floor Texture spr_floor

Test It!

Press F5!

  • Turn on the spot: the sky slides across while the walls stay put
  • Look down at the floor: the tiles get smaller toward the horizon and meet the walls neatly
No sky? Use a ceiling. If you leave Sky Texture empty, you can set Ceiling Texture instead: it is cast onto the ceiling the same way the floor is cast onto the ground — good for indoor rooms. A sky, when set, always wins.
